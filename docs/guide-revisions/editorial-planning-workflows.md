# Editorial Planning: pitches, meetings and decisions (review draft)

**Status:** Draft pending editorial review.

## Submit a useful pitch

A pitch should make a potential story legible to a colleague without requiring a separate conversation. State what is happening; whom it affects; why it matters now; what remains unknown; what evidence or sources might answer the question; and what reporting effort is likely required. Choose the coverage pillar that best describes the underlying public question, not merely the institution involved.

**Example:** Rather than "UWF hosts military program," frame the public question: whether a university's expansion of military-to-civilian training is changing access to civilian employment and for whom. The institutional activity is a lead to investigate, not necessarily the story's conclusion.

The existing workflow permits a [submitted pitch](/editorial) to be edited until it enters [meeting scoring](/editorial/meetings); use review history to understand whether it was assigned, deferred or archived.

## Run a planning meeting

1. Editors create the meeting and select a rubric appropriate to the mix of coverage. Add eligible pitches to the slate.
2. Reviewers score independently against the published anchored criteria and record substantive concerns. An institutional alignment modifier must not substitute for the story's civic value, evidence or feasibility.
3. After scoring closes, consider the ranked agenda, disagreements and written comments. Scores inform editorial judgment; they do not make the assignment decision themselves.
4. Record the disposition and assignment clearly. A deferred pitch should have an understandable next step where possible; an assigned pitch should identify the responsible reporter.
5. Conclude the meeting only after decisions are recorded. The concluded meeting is a permanent historical record.

## Move from pitch to story plan

An assignment needs more than a reporter's name. Define the central reporting question, reporting steps, essential sources, verification needs, likely format, constraints and next editorial checkpoint. Avoid presenting a hypothesis as an established finding.

## Administration

Configuration guides should explain the difference between weighted core criteria and separate modifiers, how archived rubric elements preserve historical scores, and when changing criteria is justified. Any numerical example must match the live rubric.

**Review before publication:** Verify existing pitch form fields, access rights and labels. Editorial expectations above are proposed instructional guidance, not a newly adopted binding newsroom policy.
